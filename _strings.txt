Why this clinic exists.
Two people who have spent their working lives around animals got tired of one thing: a system where the animal is an it, the owner is a wallet, and nobody has ten minutes.
The story
One of us has run a veterinary clinic in Gujarat for over a decade. The other runs a consult practice and comes from a family of practitioners. We are business partners. We have argued about this clinic for two years before opening it.
What we kept coming back to is a gap. A dog in Jaipur can get a diagnosis, a drug and a discharge. What it cannot get is the eight weeks after: the movement work, the diet, the follow-up call, the vet who remembers the case. That gap is where most animals lose their recovery.
ekam vets is built to close it. ekam means one. One roof, one file, one team, one animal at the centre of it.
We chose Vidyadhar Nagar because we want to be the clinic that people walk to, not the one they drive across the city for on the worst day of their year.
What we believe
Four things, and we are hard about them
A pet is a family member
Not an it. We use your animal's name, we ask you how you are doing when the news is bad, and we do not talk over you.
The best vet sometimes says no
No to the surgery. No to the test. No to the third medicine. A clinic that only ever says yes is selling, not treating.
Money is discussed early
Before the treatment, out loud, with a number. The awkward conversation happens at the start here, not at the counter afterwards.
A report is a fact
We do not manipulate lab values in any direction, for any reason. If that costs us a case, it costs us a case.
The team
Who you will meet
A senior veterinary surgeon leads the clinical side. A second vet handles day consultations. There is a veterinary assistant, a trained groomer, and a person at the front desk whose actual job is to make sure nobody sits confused in the waiting room.
The clinic runs under a hospital name rather than a doctor's name. That is a deliberate choice. You are trusting a practice and a standard, not one person's reputation, and the standard does not change on the day one of us is on leave.
We will look after your animal. And we will show you the bill before we start.

If you find an injured animal
What to do in the first ten minutes
1. Keep yourself safe
A frightened animal in pain bites the person helping it. Use a towel or a cloth over the head. Do not put your face near it.
2. Do not give food, water or medicine
Not water either, if the animal is barely conscious or the injury is to the mouth or abdomen. Never a human painkiller. Paracetamol kills cats.
3. Move it flat and call ahead
Slide a board, a cloth or a flattened carton under the animal and keep the spine straight. Call us on the way so a table is ready.

Before your first visit.
Straight answers. If your question is not here, call the clinic and ask. Nobody at this front desk has been told to avoid a question.
The visit
Do I need an appointment?
No. Walk in during clinic hours. Booking a slot means less waiting, and on Saturday it means a lot less waiting.
What should I bring?
The vaccination card, any previous reports and prescriptions, and the current food packet or a photo of it. If your animal is on medicine, bring the strip. A dog on a leash, a cat in a carrier.
How long does a consult take?
Plan for thirty minutes. A first consult or a complicated case takes longer. We do not run a five-minute queue, which is also why we ask you to book.
Can I stay with my animal?
Yes, through the examination and through most procedures. In surgery, no. For an X-ray we will ask you to step out or to wear the apron, depending on the case.
Do you see cats properly?
Yes. Cats get a separate waiting corner and a quieter table. If your cat panics in a queue, tell the front desk and we will take you in a side room.
Do you treat rabbits, birds and small animals?
Yes for routine care. For some specialised cases we will tell you honestly that you are better off elsewhere, and where that elsewhere is.
Emergencies and hours
Are you open 24 hours?
No, and we would rather say that than put 24x7 on a board we cannot honour. Clinic hours are Monday to Saturday 9am to 8pm and Sunday 10am to 2pm. Outside those hours, call the emergency number on the visit page. If a vet can come, a vet comes. If we cannot reach you in time, we will tell you immediately which nearby hospital to drive to instead of leaving you calling a phone that rings.
What counts as an emergency?
Road accident, heavy bleeding, seizures, breathing trouble, a swollen hard abdomen, unable to urinate, snake bite, poisoning, heat stroke, whelping trouble, and any collapse. Come straight in. Emergencies are seen before appointments, every time.
Do you do home visits?
Within about five kilometres, for routine care, vaccination and old or immobile animals. Emergencies are better handled in the clinic where the oxygen, the fluids and the X-ray are.
Money
Why is your consult Rs 350 when others charge Rs 200?
Because a consult here is thirty minutes with a written plan, a free follow-up inside seven days, and a vet who will tell you not to spend money when spending it is pointless. If you want the five-minute version at a lower price, Jaipur has plenty of it and we will not pretend otherwise.
Will I know the cost before you treat?
Yes. Estimate first, treatment after. No test and no procedure is run without your yes.
Do you push tests and vaccinations?
No. If a test will not change what we do next, we say so and we skip it. Our bills carry a line for the tests we chose not to run.
Do you take pet insurance?
We give you an itemised bill and full reports, which every Indian pet insurer accepts for reimbursement. Direct cashless tie-ups are not in place yet.
How do I pay?
Cash, UPI or card. Receipts on WhatsApp if you ask.
The care itself
What does integrated holistic actually mean?
Modern veterinary medicine is the base. On top of it we add movement work, diet, and gentle supportive remedies where they help. One team, one file, one plan. It is explained in full on the
integrated care
page.
Is this instead of regular medicine?
Never. If your animal needs an antibiotic, it gets an antibiotic. Anything alternative is added alongside, and only where it is safe to add.
Is your lab reliable?
The lab runs in this building, for our patients only, and we do not accept outside samples. Nobody here edits a value in either direction. What the machine prints is what you get.
Will I see the same vet each time?
Mostly yes. When you cannot, the file is complete enough that the next vet knows your animal without asking you to repeat the last two years.
Do you sell food and products?
A small shelf of prescription diets and basics, priced at MRP. We are a clinic, not a pet store, and we will not sell you a bag you do not need.

What happens when you walk in
Six steps, in this order, every time. No animal skips the conversation.
We ask before we touch
Food, water, sleep, walks, stools, mood, and what changed in the house last month. Half of what we need is in that conversation. Bring the person who feeds the animal.
The physical examination
Hands on the animal, on the floor or on your lap if that is where it is calm. Weight, temperature, gums, joints, coat, abdomen, ears, teeth.
Tests, only if they change the answer
If a test will not change what we do next, we do not run it. If it will, we tell you what it costs before we run it, and the sample never leaves this building.
The plan, written down
Medicine, dose, diet, movement, and what to watch for. On paper, in the language you read. You leave with it in your hand, not in your memory.
The follow-up
One free follow-up within seven days on every consult. We would rather see the animal again than guess whether the plan worked.
The file stays
Ten years from now the vet in this room can see what your animal looked like at two years old. That is the whole point of one roof.
What we combine
Four strands. Medicine leads. The other three are added on top of it, never instead of it.
Modern veterinary medicine
The base, and it is not negotiable. Diagnosis, drugs, imaging, surgery, an operation theatre and in-patient beds. If your animal needs an antibiotic, it gets an antibiotic.
Traditional and gentle remedies
Where a mild, low side effect remedy works alongside the medicine, we use it. It supports the treatment. It never replaces a drug the animal needs.
Physiotherapy and movement
Laser, passive range of motion, and a home routine you can actually do. This is where most recoveries are won or lost, and where most clinics stop.
Diet and daily routine
What goes in the bowl, how often, and how much the animal moves. The cheapest intervention in the building and the one most often skipped. We give guidance, not a product to buy.
