FAQ

Before your first visit.

Straight answers. If your question is not here, call the clinic and ask. Nobody at this front desk has been told to avoid a question.

The visit

Do I need an appointment?

No. Walk in during clinic hours but you might have to wait. Booking a slot means less waiting.

What should I bring?

The vaccination card, any previous reports and prescriptions, and details and photo of the food you give. If your animal is on medicine, bring the strip. A dog on a leash, a cat in a carrier.

How long does a consult take?

Plan for thirty minutes. A first consult or a complicated case takes longer. We cannot run a five-minute queue, which is also why we ask you to book.

Can I stay with my animal?

Yes, through the examination and through most procedures. In surgery, no. For an X-ray we will ask you to step out or to wear an apron, depending on the case.

How do you see cats properly?

Yes. Cats get a separate waiting corner and a quieter table. If your cat panics in a queue, tell the front desk and we will take you in a side room.

Do you treat rabbits, birds and small animals?

Yes, for routine care. For some specialised cases we will tell you honestly that you are better off elsewhere, and where that elsewhere is.

Emergencies and hours

Are you open 24 hours?

Clinic hours are Monday to Saturday 9am to 8pm and Sunday 10am to 2pm. Outside those hours, you may call the emergency number on the visit page. If a vet can come, he/she will. If it is not an emergency/a critical case, you can come over and admit the animal. In critical cases, we will tell you immediately which nearby hospital to drive to.

What counts as an emergency?

Road accident, heavy bleeding, seizures, breathing trouble, a swollen hard abdomen, unable to urinate, snake bite, poisoning, heat stroke, whelping trouble, and any collapse. Come straight in. Emergencies are seen before appointments, every time.

Do you do home visits?

Within about five kilometres, for routine care, vaccination and old or immobile animals. Emergencies are better handled in the clinic where the oxygen, the fluids and the X-ray are.

Money

What does a consult here include?

A consult here is thirty minutes with a written plan, a free follow-up inside seven days, and a vet who will tell you not to spend money or go for surgery unnecessarily. Also, this is a holistic, multi-disciplinary clinic with added services like pathology, physiotherapy and grooming under one roof and one treatment plan.

Will I know the cost before you treat?

Yes. Estimate first, treatment after. No test and no procedure is run without your consent.

Do you push tests and vaccinations?

No. If a test will not change what we do next, we say so and we skip it. Our bills carry a line for the tests we chose not to run.

Do you take pet insurance?

We give you an itemised bill and full reports, which every Indian pet insurer accepts for reimbursement. Direct cashless tie-ups are not in place yet, but we are working towards it.

How do I pay?

Cash, UPI or card. Receipts on WhatsApp if you ask.

The care itself

What does integrated or holistic actually mean?

Modern veterinary medicine is the base. On top of it we add homeopathy, movement work, diet, and gentle supportive remedies where they help. One team, one file, one plan. It is explained in full on the integrated care page.

Is alternative medicine given instead of regular allopathic medicine?

Never. If your animal needs an antibiotic, it gets an antibiotic. Anything alternative is added alongside.

Is your lab reliable?

The lab runs in this building, for our patients only. Nobody here edits a value in either direction. What the machine prints is what you get.

Will I see the same vet each time?

Mostly yes. When you cannot, the file is complete enough that the next vet knows your animal without asking you to repeat the last two years.

Do you sell food and products?

A small shelf of prescription diets and basics, priced at MRP. We are a clinic, not a pet store, and we will not sell you a bag you do not need.
