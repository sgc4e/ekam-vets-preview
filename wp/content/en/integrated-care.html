What happens when you walk in

Six steps, in this order, every time. No animal skips the conversation.

1

We ask before we touch

Food, water, sleep, walks, stools, mood, and what changed in the house last month. Half of what we need is in that conversation. If possible, bring the person who feeds the animal.

2

The physical examination

Hands on the animal, on the floor or on your lap if that is where it is calm. Weight, temperature, gums, joints, coat, abdomen, ears, teeth.

3

Tests, only if they change the answer

If a test will not change what we do next, we do not run it. If it will, we share the costs before we run it, and the sample never leaves the building.

4

The plan, written down

Medicine, dose, diet, movement, and what to watch for. On paper, in the language you read. You leave with it in your hand.

5

The follow-up

One free follow-up within seven days on every consult. We would rather see the animal again than guess whether the treatment worked.

6

The file stays

Ten years from now the vet in this room can see what your animal looked like at two years old. That is the whole point of one roof.

What we combine

Four strands. Medicine leads. The other three are added on top of it, never instead of it.

Modern veterinary medicine

The base, and it is not negotiable. Diagnosis, drugs, imaging, surgery, an operation theatre and in-patient beds. If your animal needs an antibiotic, it gets an antibiotic.

Homeopathic medicine

Homeopathic remedies, led by plant-based medicines, prescribed as part of the treatment by our registered vet, with input from our homeopath. They support the treatment.

Physiotherapy, massage and acupuncture

Laser, passive range of motion, and a home routine you can actually do. This is where most recoveries are won or lost.

Diet and daily routine

What goes in the bowl, how often, and how much the animal moves. The cheapest intervention in the building and the one most often skipped. We give guidance, without bias for any product.
